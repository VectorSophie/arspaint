Which direction feels right for ArsPaint?

You mentioned you may have strayed — let's find the north star. Pick the vision that excites you most.

A

Faithful MS Paint remake

Strip back to simplicity. No layers, no blend modes. Just a flat canvas with the classic tools: pencil, fill, shapes, text, zoom. The appeal is nostalgia and speed — loads instantly, zero friction, everything visible at a glance. Good for pixel art, memes, quick diagrams.

Drop: layers, blend modes, lasso, transform. Keep: shapes, fill, eraser, color picker.

B

MS Paint + just enough power

Keep it feeling like Paint but add the features that make it not painful: layers, undo history, zoom, maybe basic selection. The pitch is "Paint that doesn't suck" — familiar to everyone, but you won't lose work because you can't undo 20 steps.

This is roughly where ArsPaint is now. The question is whether to polish and focus, or push further.

C

Comic / manga drawing tool

Lean into the tone layers and vector shapes already in the codebase. Target manga artists and comic creators: panel layouts, screentones, inking tools, brush textures, export to print-ready B&W. Fills a niche that Krita and CSP occupy but with a leaner Rust-native feel.

Would need: tone rendering, panel tool, B&W workflow, ink stabilization.

D

Lightweight digital painting app

Lean fully into the painting side: pressure-sensitive brush engine, custom brush shapes/textures, color mixing, more blend modes, reference image panel. A Krita-lite that actually runs snappy on low-end hardware. Target hobbyist illustrators who find Krita overwhelming.

Would need: pressure API, brush engine rewrite, color wheel, mixer.
